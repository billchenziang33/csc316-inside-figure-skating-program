# FS-Jump3D · CSC316 Blender Library

The FS-Jump3D catalog indexes 253 recordings. Axel, Salchow, toe loop, loop, flip, and Lutz each have 40 clips. The remaining 13 are combinations. Clip IDs use the anonymous skater IDs A–D and the source file numbers. The source does not label the number of rotations, so no 1A, 2A, or 3A labels were assigned from file names. The course site's 50 competition movement entries have their own catalog.

The source is [FS-Jump3D by Ryota Takeda and colleagues](https://github.com/ryota-skating/FS-Jump3D), licensed under [CC BY-NC-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-sa/4.0/). The official C3D archive includes the May 2026 corrections to Skater_A Salchow and Toeloop filenames. The SHA-256 of `00-source/c3d-corrected-2026-05.zip` is `e700d90a81061181c18d9b0019be8c799959e46e42b969263b64f1ea394f7e8e`. The web motion adaptations use the same license. The package includes the attribution and full license text.

The skater, ice, and empty stands come from the CSC316 V178 scene. The default Blender scene retains Camera, Cube, and Light. The skates adapt [Ice Skates by IsaacTheMaverick](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/623771273fd44848bd3e4e55dc358190), licensed under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). The player uses [Three.js r160 and GLTFLoader](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js), under the [MIT license](../LICENSE.THREE.txt).

1. **Round 1 · Initial retargeting.** Read 34 C3D markers at 60 fps and convert millimeters to meters. Fit the ice plane by capture session, trim invalid ends, fill internal gaps relative to the pelvis, and apply the motion to the V178 skater. Keep the original motion and missing frame ranges for comparison.
2. **Round 2 · Continuity and ice contact.** Screen pelvis-relative marker outliers with a local median and apply mild temporal smoothing. Rebuild the arms with stable segment lengths and the knees with two-segment inverse kinematics. Estimate skate orientation from the toe, heel, and forefoot plane and constrain visible runners above the ice.
3. **Round 3 · Geometry and presentation.** Check each skate against the opposite thigh, shin, and runner. Smooth the resulting separation adjustments. The side and front cameras preserve their rink-relative orientation while tracking the skater. The player retains a skate close-up. Height and distance measurements use the exported pelvis trajectory.

Each clip's `family/clip-id/` archive contains `source-markers.npz`, `record.json`, the `.blend` scenes and web GLBs, and `rN-metrics.json` for each round. The metrics contain build scripts and input hashes. `r3-mesh-audit.json` records the measured Blender file hash, visible mesh intersections, knee flexion, and minimum blade clearance. The geometry status refers to the audit of the matching Blender file. The estimated events for `Skater_A-Axel-01` are take-off at frame 132, apex at frame 145, and landing at frame 163, in a 300-frame clip at 60 fps. Round 3 had zero reverse knees in 2,393 sampled poses and zero opposite-side intersections in 1,197 mesh samples. Its minimum sampled blade clearance was about 1.94 mm.

The motions, phases, and measurements are **illustrative / simulated**. The original captures were retargeted to a generic skater, with missing frames filled and geometry corrected. The project uses no Beijing 2022 competition motion capture. Phase markers are estimates. Determining the actual edge requires more than the lowest point of the blade. The discrete mesh audit covers sampled poses, and visual checks are still needed between samples. Rounds 1 and 2 are included for comparison and can show issues corrected in Round 3.
